**This page is under construction.** The columns are settled; the
rows are not. Every cell marked TBD is a fact we have not yet
established, and a row whose source is TBD has not been checked
against anything. Treat nothing here as confirmed until its source
column says where it came from.

The [RailCom introduction](/docs/railcom/introduction/) explains
what each feature is for. The short version: a decoder that
"supports RailCom" may send only its address, and the standard
leaves most of the rest optional, so the only way to know what a
decoder does is to look it up, which is what this table is for.

The list is ordered with the most functional tested decoders at
the top: the more RailCom features a decoder has, the higher it
sits, and untested decoders come last, greyed out.

A row describes one decoder model at one firmware version, because
features arrive in updates. Where a maker documents a feature we
say so; where we have measured it on the bench we say that; where
the two disagree we will say which we believe and why.
